Subject Leadership: Making the Most of the 12 Steps Checklist
As RE leaders, we are often looking for practical ways to review provision, identify priorities and ensure our curriculum reflects the ambition of the agreed syllabus. The 12 Steps Checklist has been developed to support exactly that process, providing a clear framework for self-evaluation, curriculum development and strategic planning.Rather than being a simple compliance exercise, the checklist is designed to help leaders reflect on the quality and impact of RE across their school. It takes you through key areas of effective RE leadership, including curriculum intent, legal requirements, planning, assessment, SEND provision, staff confidence and implementation.Why use the checklist?The checklist helps you to:Evaluate current RE provision systematically.Ientify strengths and areas for development.Ensure alignment with the agreed syllabus.Support subject action planning and school improvement priorities.Build confidence when reporting to governors and senior leaders.Monitor progress over time.The Red, Amber and Green (RAG) ratings provide an easy way to identify where practice is secure and where further work may be needed.A Practical ExampleOne primary RE lead recently used the checklist while reviewing their curriculum against the new agreed syllabus.When considering Step 5: Use of Key Questions, the subject leader rated the school as Amber. Although staff were teaching engaging RE lessons, planning was often based around topics rather than the syllabus key questions. As a result, progression between year groups was not always clear.The RE lead used this finding to set a simple action:Action: Review one unit per year group and rewrite planning around the agreed syllabus key questions.By the following term:•Teachers reported greater confidence in planning.•Pupils were able to make clearer connections between units.•Progression across year groups became easier to identify.•The subject leader was able to evidence improvement through monitoring and pupil discussions.This is a good example of how a single Amber rating can lead to focused and manageable improvement.How to Get the Most from the ChecklistTo maximise its impact:Complete it collaboratively with teaching staff where possible.Use evidence such as planning, pupil voice, work scrutiny and monitoring activities.Focus initially on a small number of priority actions.Revisit the checklist annually to review progress.Share key outcomes with senior leaders and governors.A Tool for Continuous ImprovementThe most effective RE leaders are reflective practitioners. The 12 Steps Checklist provides a valuable opportunity to pause, evaluate and plan strategically. Whether you are at the beginning of implementing the agreed syllabus or refining established practice, the checklist offers a practical roadmap for ensuring that RE remains rigorous, relevant and meaningful for all pupils.Top Tip: Don't feel pressured to achieve "green" in every area immediately. The real value of the checklist lies in identifying your next steps and using them to drive sustainable improvement over time. Small, well-planned changes often have the greatest long-term impact.Check out other SACRE Resources here

Bee Loved Fashion programme launches call-out to Rochdale high schools and colleges
High schools and colleges across Greater Manchester are invited to join Bee Loved Fashion Schools, a fully funded GMCA programme that helps young people turn unwanted textiles into new fashion while building confidence, creativity and green skills. The programme is now seeking participants from Rochdale. Launching this autumn, the hands-on after-school programme will give students of all abilities the opportunity to explore the impact of fashion and textile waste, learn repair and upcycling skills, and create new items from clothing donated by their school community.Fashion with purposeStudents will take part in six weekly, one-hour sessions that combine creativity, sustainability and enterprise. The programme will help young people develop:An increased understanding of fashion and textile wastePractical skills in repair, upcycling and makingAwareness of sustainable consumption choicesConfidence, teamwork and enterprise skillsEnrichment and personal developmentEarly careers inspirationDelivery partner Future Fashion Fair CIC will bring the ideas, inspiration and technical know-how, supporting students to try skills ranging from no-sew fixes and visible mending to block printing, redesign and basic machine use. The programme will culminate in the launch of a student-run Bee Loved Fashion Shop, where participants can sell their creations and continue operating the shop after the initial training, creating a lasting legacy. There are currently no Rochdale schools signed up for this exciting opportunity.
