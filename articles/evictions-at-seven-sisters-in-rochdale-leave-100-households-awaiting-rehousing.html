Rochdale Boroughwide Housing Appoints New Chair

Rochdale Boroughwide Housing reports significant progress in rehousing residents from the Seven Sisters tower blocks, with over 600 homes now vacant following safety concerns.

By Rochdale Daily Newsdesk

More than 600 homes are currently empty in Rochdale following the decision by Rochdale Boroughwide Housing (RBH) to evict the remaining residents from the Seven Sisters tower blocks, officially known as College Bank. This decision was announced in October 2025 and has led to a significant reduction in the number of households living in the area, with only around 100 households still awaiting relocation out of approximately 760 total flats.

The eviction process, which was initially met with shock by residents, was clarified by RBH to take place over an 18-month period. As of late August 2026, 117 households have accepted offers for new accommodation, with 82 having already moved and a further 35 in the process of relocating. The remaining residents are primarily those who have not yet found suitable housing, as many have left due to what RBH describes as 'natural attrition', which includes reasons such as tenancy endings or voluntary departures.

The need for these evictions arose from safety concerns identified in a recent building survey. The survey highlighted various issues, including structural and electrical problems, water pumping system failures, roof damage, and rising costs for heating and lighting. RBH has stated that all tenants will receive support during their transition to new homes, including financial assistance for those affected. Leaseholders will have their properties purchased at market value.

This situation has been ongoing for nearly a decade, with plans for the demolition of some of the high-rises initially proposed in 2017. Although demolition was ruled out in 2023, RBH has since reopened discussions about the future of the tower blocks, stating that no final decision has been made regarding potential demolition. Local campaigners from the Save the Seven Sisters group have expressed concerns about the implications of these developments, fearing that the evictions may lead to a permanent loss of housing in the area.
