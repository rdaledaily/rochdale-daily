Halloween in Rochdale 2026: parties, pumpkins and spooky events across the borough

39 dated Halloween listings across Rochdale, Heywood, Middleton, Milnrow, Littleborough, Norden and Wardle, with prices, times, age suitability, venues and booking links.

By Rochdale Daily Newsdesk 14 Oct 2026 · 23:31 UTC

From free family fun at the Town Hall and Empire to pool parties, pumpkin carving, costume competitions and late-night entertainment, here is our area-by-area guide to the spooky season.

Halloween falls on Saturday 31 October this year, but celebrations stretch across half-term and into Sunday 1 November. There are options for young children, teenagers, families and adults, including events advertised on local Facebook pages.

Find your area below. Listings run in date order within each area; events with several dates appear at their first date. Prices are the published figures available when checked. Where hours, age limits, admission charges or booking details remain unconfirmed, we say so.

This guide brings together the public 2026 announcements we could identify and check. Private groups, unadvertised parties and later announcements mean no guide can guarantee every event. Organisers can send additions to news@rochdaledaily.co.uk.

Find a plan quickly

Free family events: Town Hall, 26 October–1 November; Empire children’s party, 27 October.

Children’s parties: Rochdale RUFC, Milnrow and Littleborough cricket clubs, BizzyBees and Wardle Club. Check the age policies below.

Older children and teenagers: Younique Talent ages 7–17; Hollingworth Lake activity morning ages 8+; Monsters of Rock for accompanied ages 14+.

A party with a quiet room: Keeley B at Butterworth Hall on 30 October.

Adults: Norden workshops, Royal Toby bingo, Town Hall horror cinema and evening live music. Decadance is separately marked as an adults-only lifestyle venue.

Before you book

Check availability and the final checkout total. “From” prices may not cover every ticket type, adult accompaniment or transaction fee. Free entry does not necessarily include food, drinks or optional activities.

Ask organisers about allergies, accessible entrances, toilets, parking, seating and essential-carer admission where these are not specified. Swimming events have their own supervision rules. A quiet room at a busy party does not mean the whole event is a low-sensory session.
