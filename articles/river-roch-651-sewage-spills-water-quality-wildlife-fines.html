651 SEWAGE SPILLS INTO THE RIVER ROCH: 3,271 HOURS OF DISCHARGES — WHAT IS IN OUR WATER AND WHO PAYS?

Seventeen outlets discharged into the River Roch in 2025. Rochdale Daily examines what residents and wildlife may be exposed to, how water is tested, why storm overflows operate, and which fines have actually been imposed.

By Rochdale Daily Investigations 12 Oct 2026 · 21:29 UTC

ROCHDALE DAILY INVESTIGATION | 12 OCTOBER 2026

The River Roch received 651 recorded storm-overflow discharges during 2025, lasting a combined 3,271 hours across 17 outlets, according to the environmental data project Top of the Poops, drawing on water-company returns submitted to the Environment Agency. Two further outlets listed in the same river table reported no discharges. Every identified operator was United Utilities.

The numbers are startling, but require careful interpretation. The 3,271 hours are added together across outlets; some will have run simultaneously. It does not mean the whole river was continuously receiving discharges for 3,271 hours. Neither the duration monitors nor the count establish the volume released, chemical or bacterial concentrations, any particular person's illness, or whether an individual discharge was unlawful.

Sources: Top of the Poops, River Roch 2025 monitoring summary, https://www.top-of-the-poops.org/waterway/united-utilities/river-roch; Environment Agency, 2025 national storm overflow annual returns, https://www.data.gov.uk/dataset/19f6064d-7356-466f-844e-d20ea10ae9fd/event-duration-monitoring-storm-overflows-annual-returns

WHERE DID THE SEWAGE ENTER?

Rochdale wastewater treatment works recorded 104 spills lasting approximately 1,503 hours — around 46% of the 3,271 combined hours. Oakenrod combined sewer overflow recorded 87 events lasting approximately 508 hours. At Heywood (Botany) treatment works the figures were 35 events and 240 hours; Sudden Valley sewer recorded 83 events and 235 hours.

Other identified outlets included Smith Bridge Road (20 events, 158 hours), Hooley Bridge (39, 140), Albert Royds Street (73, 138), Peel Lane No. 1 (43, 92) and Peel Lane No. 2 (35, 69). These outlets are along different stretches of the river and should not all be described as being in the town centre.

Figures: https://www.top-of-the-poops.org/waterway/united-utilities/river-roch

WHY DOES THIS HAPPEN? MOST ARE NOT BURST PIPES

Much of the North West uses combined sewers, carrying rainwater from roofs and roads in the same pipes as foul sewage from toilets, bathrooms and businesses. During heavy rainfall or snowmelt, the combined flow can exceed sewer and treatment capacity. Storm overflows act as designed pressure-release points, allowing diluted, often untreated sewage and rainwater into rivers rather than letting the system back up into homes. At treatment works, storm tanks store surplus flows, but may overflow when capacity is exceeded.

That explains how the infrastructure is intended to work; it does not establish that all 651 individual River Roch operations were authorised. Legal discharge depends on the actual permit, flow thresholds and conditions at the time. Equipment failure, maintenance problems, insufficient capacity or inappropriate operation can also lead to pollution breaches. It would take rainfall, flow-to-full-treatment, maintenance and permit records to determine the reason and legality of each event.

United Utilities has said that 54% of its North West sewerage network is combined, and describes plans for further capacity and rainwater separation. The Environment Agency says longstanding underinvestment and poor asset maintenance contribute to high national overflow usage. These are wider explanations, not documented causes assigned individually to each River Roch spill.

Sources: Environment Agency, what storm overflow monitors show, https://www.gov.uk/government/news/fewer-and-shorter-storm-overflow-spills-in-2025-new-monitoring-data-shows; United Utilities better rivers report, https://www.unitedutilities.com/globalassets/documents/pdf/united-utilities-better-rivers-report-2024.pdf; Environment Agency explanation, https://environmentagency.blog.gov.uk/2025/03/27/what-are-the-2024-edm-annual-returns/

WHAT DOES 'MEASURING WATER QUALITY' ACTUALLY MEAN?

Event Duration Monitors (EDMs) measure WHEN an overflow runs and HOW LONG it runs; they do not measure the litres discharged, how much faecal bacteria is present, the ammonia concentration, or whether water is safe for people to enter. National spill counts use the regulatory event-counting methodology, rather than equalling every individual sensor activation.

River health is assessed separately through Environment Agency sampling, ecological surveys and modelling. Measurements can include dissolved oxygen, ammonia, phosphate, biological oxygen demand, temperature, acidity (pH) and selected chemical contaminants. Ecologists also survey fish, invertebrates and water plants, and consider habitat modification. An overall ecological classification combines several measures, so improvement in one chemical does not automatically mean the whole river is healthy.

For designated bathing waters, officials specifically test E. coli and intestinal enterococci as indicators of faecal contamination. Ordinary rivers are not necessarily sampled for those bacteria as regular bathing-water sites. No verified River Roch-wide 2025 bacteria sampling series has been identified in the sources examined. An overflow monitor cannot be used as a certificate of safe swimming water.

Sources: Environment Agency, river classification framework, https://engageenvironmentagency.uk.engagementhq.com/understanding-englands-water-environment/widgets/155553/faqs; Environment Agency, bathing-water bacteria testing, https://environmentagency.blog.gov.uk/2025/05/15/how-the-environment-agency-monitors-and-tests-bathing-water-quality/

WHAT DO THE RIVER'S ACTUAL TESTS SHOW?

There is documented progress as well as continuing concern. In August 2026 the Environment Agency reported that phosphate quality on the River Roch (Spodden to Irwell) improved from POOR in 2019 to GOOD in the 2025 classification. It attributed the improvement to treatment upgrades and a catchment-wide permitting system requiring better phosphorus removal at wastewater treatment works. That is an important environmental gain.

However, the Environment Agency's catchment records classify the lower Roch waterbody's ecological status as MODERATE in its published records: invertebrates were moderate and aquatic macrophytes (larger water plants) poor in the 2022 assessment. The source-to-Spodden waterbody also had moderate ecological status in the recorded classification. These classifications describe river condition, not evidence that the 2025 overflows caused each shortcoming. Historical chemical failures include persistent contaminants with multiple potential sources.

Sources: Environment Agency, August 2026 reporting, https://www.gov.uk/government/news/new-data-to-drive-action-to-improve-englands-rivers-and-lakes; Environment Agency lower River Roch waterbody, https://environment.data.gov.uk/catchment-planning/WaterBody/GB112069064600; upper River Roch waterbody, https://environment.data.gov.uk/catchment-planning/WaterBody/GB112069064720

HOW MIGHT PEOPLE AND PETS BE AFFECTED?

Untreated sewage-contaminated water can contain bacteria, viruses and parasites. Exposure while swimming, paddling, retrieving a ball or handling contaminated riverbank material can increase the risk of stomach illness, skin, eye or ear infection and, in some circumstances, severe infections. Risks depend on the actual organisms and concentration, exposure route and the person involved. The monitoring totals do not show that residents of Rochdale have been made ill by any specific spill.

Children and dog walkers using the riverbank should not mistake clear-looking water or a 'not currently spilling' monitor for proof of safety. Avoid swallowing untreated river water, wash hands after contact, keep pets from drinking discoloured or foul-smelling water, and keep children away from visible sewage contamination. Contact with the river is a different issue from the quality of treated drinking water delivered to household taps: these overflow figures do NOT show that Rochdale's tap water is contaminated.

Source: UKHSA/Environment Agency open-water health guidance, https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/swim-healthy-leaflet/swim-healthy

WHAT HAPPENS TO FISH, INSECTS AND WILDLIFE?

Sewage carries organic matter and nutrients. As microorganisms break down the waste, dissolved oxygen can fall, stressing fish and aquatic insects. Ammonia can be directly toxic to fish and invertebrates at sufficiently high concentrations; excessive nutrients can encourage algal growth and alter plant communities. Repeated pulses can affect sensitive species even where a river looks normal between storms.

The River Roch's invertebrate and plant classifications warrant continued scrutiny, but attribution matters: modified channels, urban runoff, other pollution and long-lived chemicals also affect ecological health. This investigation found no verified evidence that 651 measured overflow events killed a specified number of fish or animals in the Roch in 2025. The proper test would combine event timing with upstream/downstream sampling and evidence of wildlife impacts.

Source: Environment Agency evidence of ammonia and fish mortality in a separate investigated river pollution case, https://www.gov.uk/government/news/anglian-water-fined-300-000-after-pollution-killed-5-000-fish; River Roch classification, https://environment.data.gov.uk/catchment-planning/WaterBody/GB112069064600

IS THE RIVER GETTING BETTER?

At Rochdale wastewater treatment works, recorded overflow events fell from 116 in 2024 to 104 in 2025; discharge hours fell from approximately 1,679 to 1,503. Oakenrod likewise fell from 101 spills and around 662 hours in 2024 to 87 spills and around 508 hours in 2025. These are improvements in recorded overflow frequency and duration at those sites — NOT proof that concentrations of pathogens or total sewage volumes fell by the same proportions.

Across England, storm-overflow events fell by 35% and monitored spill duration fell by 48% in 2025 compared with 2024. The Environment Agency emphasises that 2025 was unusually dry, so these comparisons cannot all be attributed to improved infrastructure.

Sources: Rochdale works historical spill data, https://sewagemap.org.uk/locations/rochdale-wwtw; Oakenrod historical spill data, https://sewagemap.org.uk/locations/oakenrod-cso; Environment Agency national 2025 figures, https://www.gov.uk/government/news/fewer-and-shorter-storm-overflow-spills-in-2025-new-monitoring-data-shows

IS UNITED UTILITIES FINED FOR SEWAGE DISCHARGES?

Yes, when regulators establish breaches. The Environment Agency can investigate permit breaches and prosecute serious offences. Courts determine criminal fines, while other cases may be resolved through environmental enforcement undertakings and restoration payments. But discharging from a permitted storm overflow during qualifying conditions is not automatically a criminal offence, and a published spill count is NOT a count of proved illegal discharges.

On 8 September 2026 United Utilities was fined £900,000, plus £62,225 prosecution costs and a £2,000 surcharge, after admitting five environmental permit offences over sewage discharges on the FYLDE COAST following a pipe failure in 2023. In a separate case, United Utilities agreed a £517,000 environmental enforcement undertaking following permit breaches affecting the RIVER TAME in Tameside. Neither was a fine for these 651 River Roch events.

Ofwat opened an enforcement case into United Utilities' wider wastewater operations in July 2024. As of the regulator's latest published investigation-status page, that case remains OPEN; opening a case is not a finding that particular River Roch spills were illegal.

Crucially, Rochdale Daily has not identified a published fine or formal penalty specifically attributable to the 651 River Roch discharges in 2025. That is NOT a finding that no breaches occurred: the Environment Agency must be asked for inspection, incident and enforcement records for each relevant permit.

Sources: Environment Agency Fylde conviction, https://www.gov.uk/government/news/water-company-fined-record-900k-after-coastline-sewage-spill; River Tame payment, https://www.gov.uk/government/news/environment-agency-secures500k-for-tame-catchment-improvements; Ofwat enforcement status, https://www.ofwat.gov.uk/investigation-into-sewage-treatment-works-and-networks/

WHEN WILL IT STOP?

United Utilities says it plans to invest more than £3 billion in storm-overflow improvements across the North West, targeting a 60% reduction in spills over the decade to 2030. In December 2025 it also announced a £4.5 million storage project at CASTLETON treatment works, expected to complete by the end of 2026 — but that scheme concerns Trub Brook and the River Irk, NOT the River Roch outlets in the 651 tally.

For meaningful local accountability, United Utilities should provide a dated, outlet-by-outlet plan for Rochdale wastewater treatment works, Oakenrod, Sudden Valley and the other River Roch overflows, with construction milestones, budgets, expected reductions and results after completion.

Sources: United Utilities investment strategy, https://corporate.unitedutilities.com/better-rivers/; Castleton project announcement, https://www.unitedutilities.com/corporate/newsroom/latest-news/united-utilities-starts-4.5m-scheme-in-castleton/

QUESTIONS FOR UNITED UTILITIES AND THE ENVIRONMENT AGENCY

Rochdale Daily is asking: (1) Of the 651 recorded River Roch events in 2025, how many were checked against rainfall and permit limits, and were any found to be unauthorised? (2) What were the estimated discharge volumes and upstream/downstream E. coli, enterococci, ammonia and dissolved-oxygen measurements near Rochdale works and Oakenrod? (3) What fish kills, wildlife incidents or public health complaints have been linked by regulators to the relevant outlets? (4) Which sites were inspected and what warnings, civil penalties, enforcement undertakings or prosecutions resulted? (5) Exactly which River Roch upgrades have been funded, when will they be completed and how will the effect be measured?

Residents witnessing suspected sewage or dead fish can report water pollution to the Environment Agency's 24-hour hotline, 0800 80 70 60, or its online report-water-pollution service. Note the date, location, smell, colour and any visible debris; do not enter dangerous water to gather evidence.

Source: https://www.gov.uk/report-water-pollution

THE BOTTOM LINE

Six hundred and fifty-one recorded sewage discharges are an important indicator of pressure on the River Roch's sewerage system, not 651 proven crimes. There is measurable progress in phosphate and at some monitored sites; there is also a compelling need for bacteriological data, permit compliance evidence and outlet-level investment results. Residents deserve those answers before being told the river is either safe or beyond recovery.
