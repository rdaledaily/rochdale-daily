Pacific, Mangos and Empire until morning: remembering Rochdale’s strip

Box-dye red hair, Jägerbombs and taxi queues that lasted hours—remembering Rochdale’s strip, its familiar faces and the venues keeping the town alive today.

By Rochdale Daily Newsdesk 14 Oct 2026 · 15:31 UTC

Box-dye red hair, Jägerbombs and taxi queues that lasted hours—Rochdale’s nightlife in the 2000s and early 2010s was a whole chapter of growing up. And places such as Empire are still helping the town make memories.

There was a time when a night out in Rochdale came with its own wardrobe, soundtrack and familiar faces.

Box-dye red hair. Chinos. Ed Hardy. The infamous Rihanna jumper. Outfits that, looking back, were remarkably close to business casual—worn with complete confidence for a night on the strip.

Rihanna and Eminem provided the soundtrack. Ordering a Jägerbomb felt like an event. Pacific, Mangos, Litten Tree and Empire were names woven into the weekend.

Between the bars, the dancefloors and the eventual search for a taxi, Rochdale felt wonderfully alive.

One former glass collector and barmaid remembers an incredible scene: heaving venues, wonderful bouncers and, in her experience, few fights. When the night finally ended, queues for taxis could mean waiting for hours into the early morning.

The people who made the strip

Ask someone about their old nights out and they might start with a club. Before long, they will be talking about a person.

A favourite barmaid. A reassuring face on the door. Someone you always bumped into, whether you had arranged to meet or not.

Among those familiar faces was the man affectionately known to revellers as “the gas man”, remembered sitting at the corner of the strip.

The former bar worker recalls people stopping to hug him and give him money. Despite the difficult circumstances she remembers him facing, he was a beloved presence, recognised and greeted with affection.

His circumstances were his own, and a nickname cannot tell his whole story. But the warmth with which he is remembered belongs in the history of those nights, alongside the music and the venues.

The strip’s character came from its people.

From the glass collector’s side of the dancefloor

Working through those nights meant seeing what customers sometimes missed.

Collecting glasses through a crowded room. Keeping the bar moving. Watching the door team manage another wave of arrivals while friends tried to find one another inside.

The former worker remembers the bouncers warmly. Her account offers a different perspective from the assumption that a busy nightlife scene must have been defined by trouble: what stays with her is how enjoyable it was, how few fights she encountered and how much life filled the town centre.

That is a personal recollection, rather than a claim that every night passed without incident. But it captures something worth preserving—the pleasure of working somewhere everyone wanted to be.

What happened to Pacific, Mangos and Litten Tree?

Pacific, at 8 South Parade, is now listed as closed. An archived council entry records a premises-licence suspension beginning in July 2013 for non-payment of an annual fee or levy. That record does not, by itself, establish the venue’s final trading date or the full reasons it closed.

Sources: https://licensing.rochdale.gov.uk/1/LicensingActPremises/Search/204/Detail?APP_ID=80078 and https://www.pubsgalore.co.uk/areas/rochdale-central/greater-manchester/

Mangos’ connection to a later venue remains visible in the paperwork. The council’s licensing record for Trinity retains conditions referring to the operating plan for Mango Bar at 3 King Street. The familiar address acquired another identity, although the sources reviewed do not establish the complete changeover story.

Source: https://licensing.rochdale.gov.uk/1/LicensingActPremises/Search/203/Detail?LIC_ID=6700

Litten Tree’s premises at 10 Fleece Street subsequently became Yates and are now Courtyard Rochdale. CAMRA records its reopening under the Courtyard name in March 2024. For former regulars, the sign has changed, but the address remains somewhere people can meet for a night out.

Source: https://camra.org.uk/pubs/courtyard-rochdale-rochdale-153361

Empire: the night that kept going

Then there was Empire.

In the former bar worker’s memories, it was the infamous destination where the night could stretch towards 6am. Leaving meant discovering that the early morning had arrived while everyone was still enjoying themselves.

Empire remains part of Rochdale’s nightlife today. Its presently advertised Friday and Saturday hours run until 4am, with live bands and performances central to its programme.

Source: https://empirerochdale.co.uk/

Its contribution extends beyond an ordinary weekend. The council’s 2026 Feel Good Festival programme included performances throughout the day at Empire, followed by a midnight DJ slot.

Source: https://www.rochdale.gov.uk/rochdale-feel-good-festival/indoor-venues

That continuing activity deserves recognition. Venues such as Empire give people a reason to come into Rochdale, meet friends, hear music and spend an evening in their own town.

The old strip cannot be recreated exactly. Its customers have grown older; their wardrobes, responsibilities and stamina may have changed.

But the affection remains—for the songs, the people, the staff who looked after everyone and the nights nobody wanted to finish.

Rochdale’s next great night out needs people willing to make it happen. Empire and the town’s other active venues are keeping that possibility alive.

What do you remember about the strip? Pacific or Mangos? Litten Tree before Empire? The Rihanna jumper, the Jägerbombs or the seemingly endless taxi queue? Share your memories—and give a mention to the bar staff, glass collectors and door teams who made those nights special.
