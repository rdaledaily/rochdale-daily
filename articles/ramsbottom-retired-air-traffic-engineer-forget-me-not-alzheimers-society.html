From air traffic control to author: Ramsbottom retiree supports dementia charity

After 36 years working in air traffic control engineering, Ramsbottom resident Ged has published his debut novel Forget Me Not, with profits going to Alzheimer’s Society and around £700 raised so far.

By Rochdale Daily Newsdesk 13 Oct 2026 · 19:25 UTC

After a 36-year career as an engineer working in air traffic control, Ramsbottom resident Ged has found an unexpected new chapter in retirement: becoming a novelist and raising money for people affected by dementia.

His debut novel, Forget Me Not, was released on 3 October 2026. Inspired by real events, the family drama follows Vera, a 91-year-old woman living with dementia, and the relatives whose responses to her illness reveal very different intentions.

At the heart of the story are difficult questions about family loyalty, protection and vulnerability. Some of Vera’s relatives want to keep her safe, while others see an opportunity to take advantage. The novel explores the pressures that dementia can place on families, and what happens when trust is tested.

Ged said the leap from engineering to fiction surprised even him. After spending more than three decades working in air traffic control, he had not expected retirement to lead to the publication of a book.

He has also given the project a charitable purpose. Ged says every penny of profit from Forget Me Not will go to Alzheimer’s Society, and that supporters have already helped raise around £700 since the novel’s release. The total is the author’s reported figure.

To make the story accessible to people who would rather borrow than buy, he has donated copies to libraries in Ramsbottom, Bury, Prestwich and Radcliffe. He hopes the books will be available for local readers to borrow soon.

Forget Me Not is available on Amazon in paperback (£9.99) and Kindle format (£2.99), with access also offered through Kindle Unlimited, according to the author’s announcement. Readers can find it at https://www.amazon.co.uk/dp/B0HJP695NC.

Signed copies can be requested through https://forms.gle/RPyfy2TbDMZZKEUX7, with free hand delivery offered around Bury. Readers can also follow updates about the book at https://www.facebook.com/forgetmenotanovel.

For those whose families have been affected by dementia, Alzheimer’s Society provides information, practical support and advice at https://www.alzheimers.org.uk/.

Ged hopes the novel will both find an audience and make a meaningful contribution to a cause close to many families across Greater Manchester.
