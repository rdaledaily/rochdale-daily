RBH tenants rate landlord in top 25 per cent nationally on all 12 survey measures

National figures from the Regulator of Social Housing place Rochdale Boroughwide Housing in the upper quartile of large landlords on all 12 tenant survey measures for 2025/26.

By Rochdale Daily Newsdesk 9 Nov 2026 · 00:00 GMTUpdated 12:35 GMT

Tenants of Rochdale Boroughwide Housing, the borough's largest social landlord, rate it among the top quarter of large landlords in England on every survey measure, new national figures show.

The Regulator of Social Housing published the 2025/26 Tenant Satisfaction Measures on Thursday 5 November, covering 356 submissions from landlords with 1,000 or more homes.

RBH set itself a target of reaching the "upper quartile", the top 25 per cent of landlords nationally, and said all 12 of its tenant survey results are now in that band.

Rochdale Daily checked each of RBH's published scores against the regulator's upper-quartile figures, which are rounded to whole numbers. All 12 clear the line.

Overall satisfaction was 80.5 per cent, against an upper quartile of 80 per cent and a national median of 74.2 per cent.

Satisfaction with repairs was 85.2 per cent (upper quartile 82 per cent), with the time taken to complete a repair 80.4 per cent (79 per cent), and that the home is well maintained 82.9 per cent (80 per cent).

Tenants who said their home is safe stood at 88.2 per cent (85 per cent). Those satisfied that RBH listens and acts on their views were 76.6 per cent (72 per cent), and that it keeps them informed 85.1 per cent (79 per cent).

Agreement that RBH treats tenants fairly and with respect was 85.7 per cent, the narrowest margin, against an upper quartile of 85 per cent.

Among tenants who had complained, 48.6 per cent were satisfied with how it was handled (45 per cent). Satisfaction with communal areas was 77.2 per cent (75 per cent), with RBH's contribution to the neighbourhood 79.2 per cent (74 per cent), and with its handling of anti-social behaviour 74.4 per cent (68 per cent).

In a statement, RBH said: "We are very proud to let you know that, across all 12 tenant satisfaction measures covered by our survey, our customers have placed our performance among the top landlords in the country."

It added: "We have only been able to improve by using the feedback and experience from our customers alongside the hard work and expertise of our team and the support of our stakeholders."

"We're pleased that our customers are telling us we're moving in the right direction, and we're determined to continue to learn and continue to improve," the statement said.

The 12 measures come from a telephone survey carried out for RBH by the independent firm Viewpoint. Every social landlord must ask the same questions, set by the regulator.

The regulator advises comparing landlords that used similar survey methods. Telephone is the most common method nationally, used for 65 per cent of surveys of rental tenants.

RBH's claim covers the 12 survey measures only. The regulator also publishes 10 measures drawn from landlords' own records, where RBH's picture is more mixed.

RBH completed 99.6 per cent of required gas safety checks, below the national median of 99.9 per cent. It answered 93 per cent of stage one complaints within the Housing Ombudsman's timescales, just under the median of 93.8 per cent.

It recorded 29.7 stage one complaints per 1,000 homes, about half the national median of 56.8, and reported that 0.2 per cent of its homes fail the Decent Homes Standard, against a median of 0.6 per cent.

Every RBH score improved on the previous year. Overall satisfaction rose from 76.6 per cent in 2024/25.

The results come nearly six years after the death of two-year-old Awaab Ishak, who died in December 2020 after prolonged exposure to mould in an RBH flat.

The regulator downgraded RBH's governance to a non-compliant G3 rating in December 2022. It restored the landlord to a compliant G2 rating in March 2025, after two years of intensive engagement.

RBH's full 2025/26 results and survey method are published on its website, and the regulator's national figures for every large landlord are available on GOV.UK.

The earlier version also said all social landlords took part; the national figures cover 356 submissions from landlords with 1,000 or more homes.
