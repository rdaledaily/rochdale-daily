FIVE YEARS LATER, A COURT SUMMONS: Rochdale residents report historic Council Tax demands as arrears reach £28.4m

Several residents tell Rochdale Daily they have received summonses for old Council Tax bills, including a 2021 account. With arrears reportedly £28.4m, we examine recovery rules, compare council finances, and list free debt help.

By Rochdale Daily Investigations 12 Oct 2026 · 21:51 UTC

ROCHDALE DAILY INVESTIGATION | 12 OCTOBER 2026

Several residents have approached Rochdale Daily reporting that they have received court summonses over Council Tax bills from previous years. One account relates to a bill dating back to 2021 — approximately five years ago. These reports are residents' accounts; the paper has not independently examined the full Council Tax and court history for every case.

For households already struggling with essentials, an old demand coupled with court costs can create significant additional pressure. The issue raises questions about whether Rochdale Council has changed its approach to historic arrears, what notices were originally sent, how payments are allocated and what support is offered before enforcement.

ROCHDALE'S £28.4 MILLION COUNCIL TAX ARREARS

Government collection returns for 2025/26, as compiled by Council Tax Helpline, place Rochdale's total Council Tax arrears outstanding at around £28.4 million at 31 March 2026, including recovery-related costs — up approximately 12.7% over the year. This is not the amount owed by any individual and should not be divided among residents as if everybody owes it.

Rochdale Council's draft 2025/26 accounts use a different accounting measure. Their Council Tax debtor balance increased from £19.585 million at 31 March 2025 to £21.958 million at 31 March 2026. Those figures should not be added to the £28.4 million or treated as directly interchangeable; they reflect different financial reporting definitions.

Sources: government 2025/26 collection returns https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/collection-rates-for-council-tax-and-non-domestic-rates-in-england-2025-to-2026; authority comparison https://www.counciltaxhelpline.co.uk/research/council-tax-arrears/; council draft accounts, Note 15 https://rochdale.moderngov.co.uk/documents/s122950/Append.%201%20for%20Draft%20Statement%20of%20Accounts%20202526.pdf

CAN THE COUNCIL SUMMON SOMEONE FOR A 2021 BILL IN 2026?

Potentially, yes. Regulation 34(3) of the Council Tax (Administration and Enforcement) Regulations 1992 generally gives a council six years from the date the sum became due to bring proceedings for a liability order. A bill described as relating to 2021 is not automatically outside that period in October 2026; the precise demand and due dates matter.

A previously granted liability order changes the position, because subsequent enforcement is not necessarily subject to the same six-year limit for starting the original proceedings. Anyone disputing liability or the timetable needs their account history, copies of notices, and dates of any earlier court orders.

Source: Council Tax (Administration and Enforcement) Regulations 1992, Regulation 34 https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/1992/613/regulation/34

WHAT ARE THE COURT COSTS — AND WHAT SHOULD YOU DO?

Rochdale Council's court-hearings guidance says a summons may carry court costs of £101.50 or £102. If the council obtains a liability order, enforcement fees may increase what someone has to pay. A summons is not proof that the amount claimed is accurate: residents can challenge liability or payment records through the proper process.

Residents should NOT ignore a summons. Ask immediately for the annual bill, statutory reminders, itemised transactions and credits, discounts and exemptions, dates notices were sent, and confirmation of whether a liability order already exists. If challenging liability before magistrates, follow the court-hearing telephone attendance instructions on Rochdale Council's website and the summons itself. Financial hardship alone is not normally a defence to liability, but it is a reason to seek an affordable arrangement and specialist debt advice without delay.

Source: Rochdale Council court-hearing guidance https://www.rochdale.gov.uk/council-tax/problems-paying-council-tax/3; contact Council Tax 0300 303 8870 or council.tax@rochdale.gov.uk

COULD PAYMENTS BE GOING TO DIFFERENT TAX YEARS?

Rochdale Council explains that its software normally allocates a payment matching a current-year instalment to the current year. A payment matching more than one such instalment can also be allocated to the current balance rather than old arrears. Other payments may go to the oldest debt. Residents seeking to pay arrears for a specific year are advised to tell the council explicitly which year's debt the payment is intended to reduce.

That does not establish an error in any resident's case, but it makes a full payment allocation history essential when a debt resurfaces years later.

Source: Rochdale Council, 'How payments are allocated' https://www.rochdale.gov.uk/council-tax/pay-council-tax

IS THIS A NEW HISTORIC-DEBT CRACKDOWN?

The public records do not prove that. The council was collecting older arrears before 2026. In October 2023 it reported collecting approximately £2.5 million in Council Tax arrears by the end of that August, about £400,000 more than in the comparable prior period.

To establish whether residents are receiving more summonses for old debts now, Rochdale Daily is seeking year-by-year summons figures broken down by the year of original liability, amounts claimed, recovery costs, cancelled summonses and changes in recovery policy. We have not found such a breakdown in publicly accessible reporting.

Source: Council meeting reports, October 2023 https://rochdale.moderngov.co.uk/documents/g6401/Public%20reports%20pack%2018th-Oct-2023%2018.00%20Council.pdf?T=10

AND WHAT ABOUT THE £50 MILLION UPPERBANKS LOANS?

Rochdale Council's draft accounts show £50.309 million in contractual loans owing from council-owned companies associated with the Upperbanks hotel and residential development at 31 March 2026. During 2025/26 the loans accrued approximately £2.020 million in interest and recorded £132,000 in repayments. Their loan arrangements include payment holidays and other contractual conditions.

These are NOT unpaid household taxes and the size of a commercial loan balance does not, by itself, imply it is overdue or in default. Crucially, no evidence has been established connecting Upperbanks lending with the pursuit of old Council Tax bills. The legitimate point of comparison is whether the council makes its debt-management decisions transparent across both areas of public finance.

Source: Rochdale Council draft 2025/26 accounts, loan disclosure https://rochdale.moderngov.co.uk/documents/s122950/Append.%201%20for%20Draft%20Statement%20of%20Accounts%20202526.pdf

FREE HELP IF YOU CANNOT AFFORD YOUR COUNCIL TAX

StepChange Debt Charity: 0800 138 1111, free advice online at https://www.stepchange.org. Advisers can help prioritise Council Tax arrears, assess household finances, work with creditors and consider suitable debt solutions.

National Debtline: 0808 808 4000, https://nationaldebtline.org. Independent help with Council Tax, summonses, liability orders, enforcement agents and budgeting.

Citizens Advice Rochdale: 0808 278 7803. Rochdale advice sessions include Number One Riverside, Smith Street. Check current drop-in and outreach arrangements at https://www.citizensadvicesort.org.uk/offices/rochdale. Support includes benefits, housing, debt and practical assistance.

MoneyHelper: find free regulated debt advice and budgeting guidance at https://www.moneyhelper.org.uk/en/money-troubles/dealing-with-debt/debt-advice-locator.

Rochdale Council Tax team: 0300 303 8870, council.tax@rochdale.gov.uk, https://www.rochdale.gov.uk/council-tax. Request an itemised statement, challenge inaccurate liability through the prescribed route, discuss affordable payment arrangements and ask about Council Tax Support.

COUNCIL TAX REDUCTIONS AND EMERGENCY SUPPORT

Rochdale residents on low incomes may qualify for Council Tax Support. Single-person discounts, disability-related reductions and statutory exemptions can apply where conditions are met. The council also has a discretionary Council Tax discount policy under Section 13A of the Local Government Finance Act 1992 for certain exceptional circumstances. Ask the Revenues and Benefits team what evidence is required.

Source: Rochdale Council discretionary Council Tax policy, updated March 2026 https://www.rochdale.gov.uk/directory-record/2426/discretionary-council-tax-discount-policy; Council Tax services https://www.rochdale.gov.uk/council-tax

Rochdale's Crisis and Resilience Fund supports eligible residents facing financial emergencies, including certain emergency payments, housing assistance and essential items. It does not automatically settle Council Tax arrears, but may relieve pressure on everyday essentials. Cost of Living Services: 01706 923685. Details at https://www.rochdale.gov.uk/cost-living/crisis-resilience-fund.

Some people can obtain a 'Breathing Space' debt respite through an authorised debt adviser. Standard Breathing Space can provide up to 60 days' protection from most enforcement action and interest or charges on qualifying debts, subject to eligibility. A separate scheme exists for qualifying mental health crisis treatment. It does not write off debts. Government guidance: https://www.gov.uk/options-for-dealing-with-your-debts/breathing-space.

SIX QUESTIONS FOR ROCHDALE COUNCIL

1. How many summonses issued in 2025 and 2026 related to Council Tax liabilities originating in 2021 or earlier?

2. Has any targeted exercise or policy change been introduced to recover older Council Tax debts, and when?

3. How much has been added to old balances through court and enforcement costs, and how much was actually collected?

4. How many summonses have been withdrawn because of incorrect liability, missing payment credits, defective notices or limitation issues?

5. What safeguards are used where residents disclose disability, serious illness, unemployment or acute financial hardship?

6. How many residents received repayment arrangements, Council Tax Support or exceptional discounts before enforcement progressed?

Rochdale Daily invites Rochdale Borough Council to respond with records and explanations. The paper also invites residents to share experiences of historic summonses confidentially at news@rochdaledaily.co.uk. Do not send personal financial reference numbers or identifiable court documents publicly; redact sensitive information before sharing.

THE BOTTOM LINE

Council Tax is a lawful obligation, and the council can pursue valid arrears. Residents are equally entitled to a clear statement of what they owe, when it became payable, which notices were served, how every payment was credited and what help is available if they cannot pay.

Rising arrears are documented. A new campaign of historic summonses, or a connection to Upperbanks, has not yet been demonstrated. Obtaining the age breakdown of court cases is the next test of the accounts residents have brought to Rochdale Daily.

General information only; individual debt cases require prompt, tailored advice and attention to any court deadline.
