The children left waiting: has Rochdale cleared its 1,400-case SEND backlog?

After an Ombudsman identified more than 1,400 delayed SEND review decisions, Rochdale Council challenged the figures and reported improvements. Have affected families received the support they were owed?

By Rochdale Daily Investigations 12 Oct 2026 · 22:20 UTC

For parents raising a child with special educational needs, an Education, Health and Care (EHC) plan is far more than paperwork. It sets out the provision a council must secure, and can determine the specialist teaching, therapies and support a child receives. When it is not reviewed or amended on time, families can be left uncertain about whether their child's needs are being met and when they can challenge decisions.

In March 2025 the Local Government and Social Care Ombudsman publicised an investigation identifying widespread delays in Rochdale Borough Council's handling of annual reviews. The council later disputed the number reported, while also reporting substantial improvements to its service. More than 18 months after the public findings, a critical question remains: can the council demonstrate that the historic cases have been resolved and that any lost provision has been addressed?

WHAT THE OMBUDSMAN FOUND

The Ombudsman's report, decision reference 24 010 066 dated 10 February 2025, followed concerns about delayed updates to children's EHC plans. It found fault in the council's delays sending decisions after annual reviews and in issuing amended plans.

According to the Ombudsman's March 2025 account, the council had more than 1,400 cases in which it had failed to tell parents within four weeks whether an EHC plan would be amended, maintained or ceased. In 600 cases the council had decided to maintain or cease plans, leaving families waiting longer than they should have to exercise potential appeal rights. Among 300 cases where the council decided to amend a plan, nearly 100 children and young people were still waiting for a draft plan after six months.

The number of completed reviews for which updated EHC plans had not been issued rose from more than 800 in June 2024 to more than 1,300 by October 2024. These figures represent different stages of the process; they must not be added together or treated as proof that every child lost education. They nevertheless show the scale of missed statutory timescales.

THE COUNCIL CHALLENGED THE 1,447 FIGURE

The council's own account subsequently differed sharply from the headline number. Minutes of the Health, Schools and Care Overview and Scrutiny Committee on 23 July 2025 record the Head of SEND saying a misinterpretation of data by the Ombudsman had led to an incorrect report of 1,447 overdue reviews. Councillors were told there had been 808 overdue reviews as of December 2024.

The same minutes recorded the council's position that 75% of its annual-review outcomes had been communicated within four weeks in 2024, compared with a national figure of 44.4%. The council said an improvement action plan had been implemented in May 2025 and was reviewed monthly.

There may be differences in the dates, definitions or stages of cases counted in the two figures. The published minutes do not reconcile them in detail, and they do not by themselves overturn the Ombudsman's formal findings. Families deserve a clear explanation of precisely what each total counted.

IMPROVEMENTS REPORTED — BUT DID THE OLDEST CASES CLOSE?

The council's December 2025 portfolio reporting provided evidence of progress. It recorded that 96% of annual reviews were receiving responses within four weeks in the latest reported quarter, compared with 63% previously. The council said it had 3,704 active EHC plans in November 2025 and separately reported completion of its Ombudsman annual-review improvement action plan.

Those are significant improvements, but a high rate of new decisions made on time is not necessarily the same as clearing the older backlog. The reports reviewed for this investigation do not establish, as at 12 October 2026, exactly how many cases identified during the original Ombudsman investigation remained unresolved, nor the length of any remaining delays.

A FURTHER UPHELD COMPLAINT

An Ombudsman decision dated 25 June 2026 illustrates what prolonged delays can mean to an individual family. In case 25 004 481, a child's EHC plan had been reviewed in May 2024 and should have been amended by August 2024. The final amended plan was not issued until August 2025, around 12 months late.

The child had been permanently excluded from school in January 2025. Although the council arranged a place at a pupil referral unit, the Ombudsman found that it had failed to retain adequate oversight of whether the alternative arrangements met the child's needs. It also found poor communication and complaint handling. The decision recorded that the council apologised and offered a financial remedy.

This decision, published in June 2026, largely concerned events during 2024 and 2025. It should not be presented as proof that the original backlog persisted at the same level in June 2026. It does, however, show the practical consequences that can follow when plans and provision are not kept under proper review.

HAVE FAMILIES BEEN REMEDIED FOR LOST SUPPORT?

In another upheld case, decision 23 009 491 dated 30 September 2024, the Ombudsman found Rochdale had failed to amend a young person's plan after a December 2021 review, failed to complete subsequent reviews and failed to secure specified special educational provision. Remedies included payments for missed provision.

The existence of individual remedies raises a wider public-interest question: did the council assess other children affected by the systemic delays to determine whether education, therapies, specialist provision or opportunities to appeal had been lost? The original backlog figure cannot be used as a count of children who missed schooling, and no such figure is established by the records examined here.

ROCHDALE DAILY ASKS THE COUNCIL TO RESPOND

On 12 October 2026, Rochdale Daily emailed the council's published Corporate Policy, Performance and Improvement address and asked for the questions to be passed to its SEND service and Media Office. The council has been invited to comment on three key points:

First, why do the public records contain figures of 1,447 and 808 overdue reviews, and can the council reconcile the measures, definitions and dates behind them?

Second, what are the latest verified figures for outstanding EHC annual-review decisions and amended plans as of October 2026, including cases beyond statutory deadlines and the oldest unresolved cases? How many of those identified in the original Ombudsman report have been completed?

Third, has the council assessed whether families affected by delayed plans missed educational provision, therapy, specialist support or appeal opportunities; how many cases were assessed; and how many families received redress or compensation, and of what total value?

The council has also been asked what improvements have followed the completed action plan. This invitation is open. Any substantive response will be reported fairly and the article updated. No response had been received by the time this report was prepared; the questions were sent on the evening of 12 October.

The issue is not only whether Rochdale completed an action plan. It is whether the children and young people whose reviews were delayed received the support they were legally entitled to, and whether families have been told what happened to their cases.

HELP FOR AFFECTED FAMILIES

Rochdale Council's confidential SEND Advice Line can be reached on 0300 303 0380 or at SENDAdviceLine@Rochdale.Gov.UK. Barnardo's Rochdale SENDIASS provides free impartial advice on EHC plans, annual reviews and appeals on 01706 769634, or via rochdale.sendiass@barnardos.org.uk. Parents should keep copies of their annual-review paperwork, dates and correspondence, and seek independent advice promptly because appeal deadlines can be short.

Rochdale Daily welcomes confidential accounts from families affected by delayed SEND support at news@rochdaledaily.co.uk. Please do not send sensitive information about a child until a secure method of sharing it has been agreed.
